Mesh Nodes vs. Hardwired Access Points

Most people add another mesh node. That rarely solves it. Here's why hardwiring is the permanent fix.

❌ Why Mesh Nodes Fail

  • Nodes wirelessly backhaul to each other
  • Each hop cuts bandwidth in half
  • Interference from walls & appliances
  • Congestion during peak usage
  • Still get dead spots in corners
  • Ongoing subscription fees

✅ Hardwired Access Points

  • Cat6 cable runs to each AP location
  • Full gigabit to every access point
  • No wireless backhaul interference
  • Ceiling mount = full 360° coverage
  • One unified SSID, seamless roaming
  • One-time cost, no subscription

WiFi Dead Zone FAQ

Common questions before booking a site survey.

Can't I just buy a WiFi extender or another mesh node?

You can, but wireless extenders repeat a weaker signal — you get about half the speed of what you already have. Mesh nodes with wireless backhaul have the same problem. Hardwired access points receive full gigabit via Cat6 cable and broadcast a strong, fresh signal. It's a permanent fix, not a band-aid.

What access point brands do you install?

We typically install Ubiquiti UniFi, TP-Link EAP (Omada), or EnGenius access points — all enterprise-grade hardware that runs circles around consumer mesh systems. We can also install hardware you already own.

Do I need to replace my router?

Usually no. We connect the access point(s) to your existing router via Cat6. If your router is the bottleneck, we'll tell you, but most routers work fine with added hardwired APs.

How long does the installation take?

A single access point install usually takes 2–3 hours including cable run, mounting, and configuration. Multiple APs may take a half day.
